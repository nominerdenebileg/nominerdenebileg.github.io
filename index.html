Selected work

Design, fabrication, and flight — from CAD to the launch pad.

Starlance Avionics Bay Assembly (Onshape)

Designed and modeled the full avionics bay for Starlance, our IREC 2025 competition rocket. Every section has a job — dual Stratologgers and an SRAD flight computer for reliable dual deployment, three onboard cameras and air-data sensors in the chamber, and a dedicated Li-Ion battery bay powering live telemetry and video back to our ground station.

Waterjet-Cut & Machined Fiberglass Fins

Cut Starlance's fins in-house from 0.25 in G10 fiberglass using a waterjet, then took them to the mill to shape a true airfoil profile, chamfered at 7° down to 1 inch from the edge — a reminder that aerodynamics starts on the shop floor.

L1 Rocket — Final Pre-Flight Prep

The moment before launch: wiring up ignition on my Level 1 rocket, built completely from scratch. From CAD to fabrication to this final systems check on the pad.

4th Place — IREC 2025

Placed 4th in the Student Researched and Developed 10K ft category at IREC 2025 in Midland, TX, competing against 172 university teams from around the world — an incredible experience seeing Starlance fly at the world's largest collegiate rocketry competition.

Onboard Flight Video — L1 Certification

Onboard footage from my Level 1 certification flight. You can really see the roll during ascent — lots of great data to learn from, with L2 already in the works with a focus on improving altitude and roll control.
